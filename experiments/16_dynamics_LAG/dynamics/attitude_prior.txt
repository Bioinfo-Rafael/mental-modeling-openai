

Empirical aircraft attitude dynamics (0.2-second step):

Use the following fitted relationship as approximate guidance for aircraft
attitude prediction alongside the supplied history and action context.
It is not an exact JSBSim equation or a complete state-transition model.
It does not determine position, velocity, TAS, or action IDs by itself.
The same coefficients apply to every aircraft; do not apply them to missiles.

Let q_n = [roll_deg, pitch_deg, yaw_deg] at step n, in degrees.
Let v_n = [vn_mps, ve_mps, vu_mps], in meters per second, and s_n = tas_mps.
Define wrap(a) = ((a + 180) mod 360) - 180, component by component,
where mod returns a value in [0, 360).
Define d_n = wrap(q_n - q_(n-1)) and d_(n-1) = wrap(q_(n-1) - q_(n-2)).

Construct the 24-dimensional vector x_n by concatenating, in this exact order:
  d_n                                      (3 values: roll, pitch, yaw)
  d_(n-1)                                  (3 values: roll, pitch, yaw)
  sin(pi*q_n/180)                           (3 values: roll, pitch, yaw)
  cos(pi*q_n/180)                           (3 values: roll, pitch, yaw)
  v_n                                      (3 values: north, east, up)
  v_n - v_(n-1)                            (3 values: north, east, up)
  v_(n-1) - v_(n-2)                        (3 values: north, east, up)
  s_n                                      (1 value)
  s_n - s_(n-1)                            (1 value)
  s_(n-1) - s_(n-2)                        (1 value)

Differences are NOT divided by 0.2. Trigonometric arguments are in radians.
The following coefficients already use the original units. Do not standardize
the features or multiply the predicted increment by 0.2 again.

Predicted angle increment in degrees:
  delta_q = b + transpose(B) * x_n
Predicted next attitude:
  q_(n+1) = q_n + delta_q

For the first forecast step, use the last three historical states. For later
forecast steps, use your own predicted states whenever actual history is no
longer available. Never assume any future recorded state or action is given.
Keep inferring the remaining state components and the three action IDs using
the original task instructions. This empirical prior does not replace those
predictions. Do not invent zero values for missing inputs.

Return yaw in [0, 360) and represent roll using its equivalent angle in
[-180, 180). Handle angle differences using wrap, not raw subtraction across
the 0/360 or -180/180 boundary. Pitch remains in degrees.

Under the stated heading convention and positive nose-up pitch, the aircraft's
forward axis in north/east/up coordinates is
  [cos(pitch)*cos(yaw), cos(pitch)*sin(yaw), sin(pitch)],
using radians in these trigonometric functions. This forward axis need not be
parallel to [vn_mps, ve_mps, vu_mps]. Roll does not directly change the forward
axis. Do not force velocity direction to equal nose direction or identify TAS
with a particular velocity component.

The coefficients were fitted jointly to all three source recordings, including
the evaluated recording. They provide an in-dataset empirical prior, not
independent evidence about unseen recordings. They were fitted for one-step
prediction; recursive ten-step accuracy is not guaranteed. They do not include
explicit action or control-surface effects.
